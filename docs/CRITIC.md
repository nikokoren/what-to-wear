# Critic checklist (every writing round)

A critic picks the candidates the owner votes on. Read first:
`docs/BRIEF.md` (above all "Tightening"), `review/examples/<lang>.md`
(stars), `review/dont/<lang>.md` (vetoes), and
`review/rounds/2026-10-<lang>-tight/changes.md` (the owner's own edits).

Fix a plain typo or punctuation slip; otherwise copy lines unchanged.
Cut a candidate if any of these hold:

**Taste**
- Not funny, not sarcastic, merely pleasant, or restates the weather.
- Unclear on first read; too long for the screen.
- Wordplay on the forecast's words; "like your X" / „wie deine X“;
  predicting the reader's mishaps; explaining the joke; a cliché.
- A setup that restates the forecast before the joke.
- An obscure reference, or an assumption about the reader's life.

**Level**
- At On: any nag, order or jab at the reader („Wehe, du …“, "Don't
  forget …", „hab ich gesagt“). That line belongs at 11.
- At 11: nothing about the reader.

**Forecast sentences and units**
- The plain verb for the event: stops / „hört auf“, a break / „macht eine
  Pause“; never "eases" / „lässt nach“ for stopping.
- The state, not its absence: „Es bleibt kalt“, not „Es wird nicht
  wärmer“.
- No more certainty than the forecast has („können glatt sein“, „es
  droht Glatteis“); no weather-service terms it doesn't give
  („Kaltfront“).
- No odds or durations the data doesn't give ("not likely", "probably
  dry", "a short dry spell").
- Only the slot's placeholders; no preposition in front of one; a unit's
  sentence matches its slot (night says night).

**Facts**
- No fixed facts that vary: hours of daylight, "dark at five", "three
  pictures" (two or three show), dates the day doesn't guarantee.
- No clock words the change doesn't fix ("this morning", "for hours",
  "tonight"; use "at first", "later", "yet"); no "all day" under the
  drawings; no season or sky the slot doesn't guarantee ("winter", "the
  sun").
- Special days name the occasion in the line itself.
- Nothing you can't vouch for.

**Mechanics**
- English: US spelling and words ("grandma", "watch your step", "really"),
  "room" never "hallway"; periods and commas inside quotes; "?" on tag
  questions; a comma before a direct address.
- German: standard German (no „heuer“, „am Sofa“, „tagträumen“); comma
  before „aber“ and around infinitive clauses; German quotes „…“, a colon
  before direct speech; „das Gleiche“, „zu Hause“, „dieses Mal“.

**Duplicates**: two candidates with one idea (keep the better), or a
repeat of a live line's idea in the same language.

**Not a reason to cut** (owner, November 2026: the critics were too
harsh):
- The same idea in the other language, when the writers came to it on
  their own. Cut only a translation: the same joke in the same shape.
- A topic the owner vetoed once. A veto may have been about the wording,
  not the idea. Cut only a near-copy of a vetoed line; otherwise keep it
  and name the vetoed line in your report, so the owner can judge.
  An idea the owner vetoed with a note against the idea itself (e.g.
  "Don't reference the drawings that much") still counts.
- A variation of a starred line, where the pool is thin or the round
  asks for variety (owner, November 2026): same joke shape or frame, a
  new image or twist. Cut only a copy or a trivial reword.
