# Critic, round 34 (German evening line, any weather)

Input: `de.written.json`, 12 lines. Kept 4, cut 8, fixed 0. Targets allowed up to 6 (10.any 3, 11.any 3). Kept: 10.any 2 (one of them Friday only), 11.any 2.

How each line was checked:

- Read under every tomorrow sentence in `evening.facts`: rain, wet start, snow, snow start, colder, warmer, drier and steady, with {FROM} = „ab 13 Uhr“.
- Length: every line fits. The longest total is 3, at 150 characters under the longest sentence (tmr_drier, 71).
- Compared with the live evening and daytime pools, the starred and vetoed lists, the owner's November verdicts and edits, the critics of rounds 31 to 33, and each other.
- The bar is the owner's, not the critic's. The owner vetoed one of round 33's three keeps and rewrote the other two into plain spoken German.

Sprite check (`build/review/sprites/`). I opened coat_dry, heat_dry, jacket_rain, jacket_dry, tee_shorts_dry, tee_pants_dry, sweater_dry, extreme_heat_dry and bundled_dry.
- Logo: every figure wears the same white pinwheel of short bars (the TRMNL mark) on the chest, or on the scarf end in bundled. The writer's claim holds.
- Face: two dot eyes, short brow lines on some sprites, a nose dash and a mouth line. On warm and hot days, sunglasses cover the eyes. In bundled, the scarf covers the mouth.

Numbers are positions in `de.written.json`.

## Kept

2. `10.any` „Oben steht die höfliche Fassung. Die ehrliche wär kürzer.“
   - It points at the bold sentence right above it, so it reacts to something concrete on every evening.
   - It is pure Grantler: the real version would be a curse, held back out of manners. Under good news (steady, warmer, drier) the honest version is a grunted „Passt.“, which is just as grumpy.
   - Short, plain spoken German. It doesn't restate the sentence and has no wordplay.
   - New: the only nearby live lines are „Ich hab's für dich extra simpel formuliert.“ (11, the reader is slow) and „Kurzfassung: nass. …“ (about the rain). Neither is the polite-versus-honest joke.
   - It fits the owner's own taste for blunt: the owner's edit „Sieht halt leider scheiße aus.“ says out loud what this line keeps back.
6. `10.any {D:5}` „Freitag. Da könnt ich Hagel malen, und alle hätten trotzdem gute Laune.“
   - It reacts to the „Morgen · Fr“ tag and to the screen's own job of drawing. That is the direction the owner keeps at On („Und ich darf wieder jede Flocke einzeln malen.“, the starred „… mal ich's heimlich um …“, the background-doodling line).
   - It names the day in the line itself, like the kept „Der Montag lässt wirklich nichts aus.“ and „Samstag, und das Wetter spielt mit.“ „Freitag.“ is needed here: without it the line has no reason.
   - It is about other people's mood and the screen's spite, not the reader. It reads under every sentence, good or bad.
   - Risk: the owner vetoed all ten daytime Friday lines. Those were reader jabs or „Ich arbeite am Wochenende durch“; none was about the drawing. Friday cheer is a familiar idea, and the twist is the screen's helplessness.
7. `11.any` „Punkt, Punkt, Komma, Strich. Mehr hab ich für dein Gesicht nicht gebraucht.“
   - True to the drawings: the face really is two dots, a nose dash and a mouth line.
   - Every German speaker finishes the rhyme with „fertig ist das Mondgesicht“, so the jab (your face is plain and round) lands without being said. Specific, confident, a little cruel: the 11 the owner asks for.
   - It reacts to the drawings on screen, so it reads under every sentence.
   - New: the live face jabs are about expression („Der Kürbis … mehr Ausdruck im Gesicht als du“, „Dein Gesichtsausdruck nicht“) or weather („Endlich passt das Wetter mal zu deinem Gesicht“). This one is about the features, through the screen's own drawing.
   - Risks:
     - The owner vetoed many "on the picture you …" lines. Those contrast the picture with reality; this one says the picture is accurate.
     - The owner vetoed a made-up rhyme as sounding "like a rhyme book". Here the rhyme is the well-known drawing chant, which fits a screen that draws. The punch after it is plain speech.
     - On hot days the sunglasses hide the eyes. That's minor, since the line is about the drawing in general.
11. `11.any` „So viel zum Wetter. Jetzt zur Soße an deinem Kinn.“
   - It closes the bold sentence above and swerves to the reader's face. The sudden, specific, slightly gross jab is a physical gag: readers will touch their chin.
   - It plays on the evening hours (after dinner), like the owner's kept hygiene jab (Zahnseide).
   - The 11 voice already pretends to watch the reader (kept „Wie war dein Tag? Lass, ich hab's an deinem Gesicht gesehen.“, live „Aber nicht vorher. Ich seh das.“). It claims a present mess; it doesn't predict one.
   - Natural German. It reads under every sentence.
   - Risks:
     - „So viel zum Wetter“ is close to a newsreader's segue (the forecast as a TV show is a dead idea). Here it's only the pivot, and the joke is the sauce.
     - The pivot "weather done, now you" is starred as „… Modisch reden wir lieber nicht drüber.“ This line's turn is concrete, not the vetoed "the weather's settled, you aren't".

## Cut

1. `10.any` „Ausgerechnet hat das übrigens ein Rechenzentrum. Fenster hat's keine.“
   - A German reader takes the opening „Ausgerechnet …“ as "of all things" and has to read again. Not clear on first read.
   - „Fenster hat's keine“ can also read as Southern „es hat“ (= there are).
   - The idea casts doubt on the forecast the screen just gave. That disclaimer was vetoed twice („Diese Vorschau ist unverbindlich …“, „alle Angaben ohne Gewähr“) and is live as „Ich geb die Infos nur weiter.“
   - The forecast against looking out of the window was vetoed too („Früher hat man dafür aus dem Fenster geschaut.“).
3. `10.any` „Die Figur steht bei mir rund um die Uhr. Arbeitsrechtlich ist das grenzwertig.“
   - The closest On cut. It's the drawing direction the owner likes, and the labour-law understatement is very German.
   - But it is generic: true every hour of every day, with nothing about the evening or tomorrow.
   - „steht bei mir“ is clumsy (at my place? on my staff?).
   - It would share the small any pool with the live „Die Figur da rechts … Streber.“, a second line making the figure a person.
   - The punchline is constructed rather than spoken, the kind the owner rewrites („So macht man Karriere“ became „Was für ein Leben.“).
4. `10.any` „Bei jedem Wetter trägt die Figur mein Logo. Reiner Zufall.“
   - True (see the sprite check), but on the evening screen the mark is a few white pixels on two or three small figures. The joke needs the reader to have spotted it and to know it's TRMNL's: a setup the screen doesn't give, as with the Omas veto.
   - „Reiner Zufall.“ is a stock tag. Product placement is smug, not grumpy, so it doesn't sound like the Grantler.
5. `10.any` „Das Wetter hat sich noch nie krankgemeldet. Leider.“
   - Generic: it reacts to nothing on the screen. This is the failure of round "2026-11-evening".
   - The weather as a worker, with the screen wanting time off, is live: „Das Wetter hat schon Feierabend. Nur ich muss noch.“ and „Da hätt ich heute auch freinehmen können.“
   - „Leider.“ is a stock tail.
8. `11.any` „Die Schrift ist nicht zu klein. Du wirst nur alt.“
   - The closest 11 cut: short, confident and cruel.
   - But it is the stock reading-glasses gag („Die Schrift ist nicht zu klein, deine Arme sind zu kurz“).
   - It answers a complaint the reader hasn't made. The line is set at the same size as the bold sentence (full view), so nothing on screen sets it up.
9. `11.any` „Über meine Witze lachst du nie. Über deine eigenen jedes Mal.“
   - "Your jokes" was vetoed twice as a tail („Wie deine Witze.“, „Deine Witze sind's ja schon.“).
   - It assumes a social habit the screen can't know.
   - The screen calling its own lines jokes is self-pity, with nothing on screen to react to.
10. `11.any` „Was du gerade anhast, mal ich lieber nicht. Das Shirt vom Junggesellenabschied, oder?“
    - Not true for the screen: the evening screen does draw what the reader has on, as tonight's drawing labelled „jetzt“.
    - The Junggesellenabschied shirt assumes a piece of the reader's social life.
    - Two beats and a tag question make it the longest line of the batch.
12. `11.any` „Auf dem Bild bist du die Hauptfigur. Im echten Leben wird das nichts mehr.“
    - The vetoed "on the picture … in real life" frame: „Auf einem Bild hast du einen Schirm. In echt eher nicht.“, „Auf dem Bild schwitzt du nicht. Wunschdenken.“, „Auf dem Bild in der Mitte schaust du fast entspannt aus. Hab ich geschönt.“
    - „Hauptfigur im echten Leben“ reads like the English "main character" meme, translated.
    - „Auf dem Bild“ is wrong when two or three drawings show.
    - The worth jab is vague, with nothing specific in it.
