# Critic notes, evening round (en)

Kept 48 of 168: 4 per forecast situation (32 of 96), 8 per joke level (16 of 48).
I read every forecast line with {FROM} filled as "from 1 pm". All 32 forecast
keeps are unchanged and under 70 characters. Six joke keeps are light fixes,
marked **fixed** with the original text.

## evening.facts.tmr_steady

- "Tomorrow is a one-outfit day." The shortest, and the only one built on "is".
- "Tomorrow's outfit works all day." Plain, with the outfit as its subject.
- "Tomorrow you can wear one outfit all day." Speaks to "you", so it reads differently in rotation.
- "Tomorrow, the same outfit works from start to finish." "From start to finish" in place of a third "all day".
- **Weakest:** "from start to finish". It repeats "works" from the second keep and is the wordiest of the four.

## evening.facts.tmr_warmer

- "Tomorrow starts cooler and warms up {FROM}." Gives both halves of the day, and fits a coat start or a T-shirt start.
- "Tomorrow it's warmer {FROM}." Says the state, short.
- "Tomorrow the temperature climbs {FROM}." A different subject and verb.
- "Tomorrow brings warmer weather {FROM}." A "brings" sentence, for variety.
- **Weakest:** "brings warmer weather". It's a little weather-presenter, but it reads fine with "from 1 pm".

## evening.facts.tmr_colder

- "Tomorrow it gets colder {FROM}, so bring something warmer." The event plus why, like the live fact lines. Names no garment.
- "Tomorrow you'll want something warmer {FROM}." Built around the reader, not the temperature.
- "Tomorrow it's colder {FROM}." Says the state, short.
- "Tomorrow the temperature drops {FROM}, so pack something warm." Temperature as subject, with a different ask at the end.
- **Weakest:** "temperature drops… pack something warm". Its "so … something warm" ending echoes the first keep.

## evening.facts.tmr_rain

- "Tomorrow it rains {FROM}, so take the umbrella." The plain version.
- "Tomorrow you'll want the umbrella. It rains {FROM}." Puts the ask first.
- "Tomorrow starts dry, with rain {FROM}. Take the umbrella." Says the morning is dry, which is why the umbrella has to be packed rather than already in hand.
- "Tomorrow there's rain {FROM}. Bring the umbrella." A "there's" sentence, and "bring" for variety.
- **Weakest:** "there's rain… Bring the umbrella". It is close to the first keep in content and order.

## evening.facts.tmr_snow

- "Tomorrow it snows {FROM}." Plain.
- "Tomorrow there's snow {FROM}." A "there's" sentence.
- "Tomorrow brings snow {FROM}." A "brings" sentence.
- "Tomorrow, snow falls {FROM}." Snow as the subject.
- **Weakest:** "snow falls". It's a little bookish, but it was the only other structure that reads right with "from".

## evening.facts.tmr_drier

- "Tomorrow starts wet, then it's dry {FROM}." Gives the order of the day, and "dry from" is the state.
- "Tomorrow starts with rain. It's dry {FROM}." Two short sentences.
- "Tomorrow you'll need the umbrella to start. It's dry {FROM}." Tells the reader what to do and matches the morning drawing.
- "Tomorrow it's wet to start and dry {FROM}." One sentence with both states.
- **Weakest:** "wet to start and dry". It's flat, and the content is close to the first keep. The situation doesn't leave many ways to say it.

## evening.facts.tmr_wet

- "Tomorrow starts with rain, so take the umbrella." Plain, and says why.
- "Take the umbrella tomorrow. The day starts wet." Puts the ask first, and still says tomorrow at once.
- "Tomorrow you'll want the umbrella from the start." One sentence built around the reader. "From the start" is not the placeholder, so the grammar holds.
- "Expect a wet start tomorrow. Take the umbrella." An "Expect" sentence, for variety.
- **Weakest:** "Expect a wet start tomorrow". It doesn't open with "Tomorrow", though the word comes early.

## evening.facts.tmr_snow_start

- "Tomorrow begins with snow." Plain.
- "Tomorrow starts with snow falling." Says it's falling snow, not snow on the ground.
- "Expect a snowy start tomorrow." An "Expect" sentence.
- "Tomorrow starts out snowy." Short, with an adjective in place of the noun.
- **Weakest:** "starts out snowy". It's close to "starts with snow falling".

## evening.jokes_10 (On)

- "I've watched tomorrow twice already. The middle drags." Tomorrow as a rerun, with a twist at the end. Works with any weather.
- "Bakers get up at four to get the day ready. I had tomorrow done by eight." A concrete scene with a smug turn at the end. **Fixed** from "…at four to get tomorrow ready. I had mine done by eight." Bakers at 4 am are getting that same day ready, so the original's logic tripped.
- "Tomorrow's outfit took me longer than today's. It had to look effortless." The screen is vain, and the twist is one everyone knows. **Fixed** from "Confession: tomorrow's outfit took me longer than today's. I wanted it to look effortless." (90 characters, cut to 73).
- "I know tomorrow's weather. The weather itself won't find out until morning." The screen gets there before the weather does. **Fixed** from "Tomorrow's weather, the night before. The weather itself won't find out until morning." (86 characters, cut to 75).
- "I've known about tomorrow since lunch. I held it until eight for the drama." The screen as showman. **Fixed** from "Confession: … I waited until eight for the drama." (86 characters, cut to 75).
- "This forecast is final. The weather can appeal in the morning." The one screen-in-charge line I kept, out of the three the writer flagged. It also admits, dryly, that the forecast can change.
- "The evening news makes everyone wait for the weather. I lead with it." Something everyone has sat through, with a short punch line.
- "I rehearse tomorrow all evening. The weather never shows up to rehearsal." The weather as an unreliable cast member.
- **Weakest:** "rehearse… never shows up to rehearsal". It's more amusing than sarcastic, and it got the last spot because the other candidates had worse problems.
- **Overlap to watch:** "since lunch" and "won't find out until morning" both start from the screen knowing early, but the punch lines differ: showmanship, and the weather not knowing its own plans.

## evening.jokes_11

- "I've seen tomorrow. You look tired in it." A short jab at looks. Confident, a little cruel.
- "Tomorrow hasn't seen you yet. Lucky tomorrow." A jab at worth. **Fixed** from "You've seen tomorrow. It hasn't seen you yet. Lucky tomorrow." I dropped the opener because it echoed "I've seen tomorrow" in the same rotation.
- "Tomorrow's a fresh start. For the weather." The reader doesn't get one. Short, and the turn is the last three words.
- "Notice: tomorrow's outfit has been chosen. Your input was considered and rejected." A fake notice about the reader's opinion. **Fixed** from "…has been chosen for you. …" (90 characters, cut to 82). Still the longest keep.
- "You've read tomorrow's forecast. Probably the most reading you'll do tonight." A specific jab at the reader's mind, hedged with "probably". **Fixed** from "…That's probably the most reading…" (84 characters, cut to 77).
- "I show you tomorrow a night early. Frankly, you need the head start." The one "you're not competent" line I kept, out of the cluster of four.
- "Tomorrow's weather is taken care of. Tomorrow's hair is on you." A jab at looks, aimed at the hair rather than the face, with a parallel structure.
- "Looking ahead to tomorrow already? Today went that well, huh?" Sarcasm about the reader's day, with no assumption about what they did.
- **Weakest:** "Today went that well, huh?". It's a familiar sarcastic frame and the least specific of the eight.

## Main cut patterns

**Forecast sentences**
- An event verb in front of "from 1 pm": "Rain moves in tomorrow from 1 pm", "Snow arrives…", "Warmer weather arrives…", "Rain is coming…", "Snow comes…", "The rain stops from 1 pm" (two tmr_drier lines), "the rain is over from 1 pm". Only state verbs survive the placeholder: "it's dry", "it rains", "it's colder".
- Odds and hedges: "will likely start wet", "Rain/Snow is likely at the start", "Tomorrow's start looks wet/snowy".
- "It …" openers: "It warms up tomorrow", "It snows tomorrow", "It's a snowy start". "It gets colder tomorrow" and the second sentence "It gets warmer/colder" are banned in `tools/style_report.py`.
- Twins: warms up / gets warmer / gets warmer (tomorrow as subject); begins / opens / kicks off with snow; "starts rainy, but dry" next to "starts wet, then dry".
- Clumsy wording: "the day warms up", "the umbrella's for the start", "There's snow to start tomorrow" (it could read as snow starting tomorrow, which is tmr_snow).

**On**
- The screen in charge of tomorrow: the writer flagged three of these ("change its mind… I didn't let it", "Memo to tomorrow… don't make me redraw you"), so only "can appeal" stays.
- A second "it's fixed, no recourse" joke ("No returns, no exchanges" repeats "final… appeal").
- "Somewhere, someone…" scenes and lines that run too long (the apps line, four billion years, the fortune teller). The fortune teller also leans on a cliché ("tall, dark stranger").
- Cut as not funny: "a whole night early… a scam", "can't sneak up on me", "looks its best the night before", "time travel… paperwork", the delivery notice (pleasant, not sarcastic).
- "Today and tomorrow… not on speaking terms" is wrong on a steady day. "Out there it's still today. In here, I've moved on." leans on "in here", the indoors smugness the owner called meh, and repeats the knowing-early idea.
- "Not one has ever asked about my day" is too close to the live "Not a single 'Thank you'".

**11**
- The "you're not competent" cluster: "get ready the night before… you read about it", "more planning than anyone… including you", "functioning adult", "someone your age has tomorrow planned", "tomorrow's excuses ready". All too long, too, and "your age" assumes a peer comparison.
- Repeats of a live line: "drawn on a better-looking model" is the live "better posture than you".
- Kept-idea twins: "Nobody changes overnight" (same as "fresh start. For the weather."), "you'll still look like that" and "your face will take most of the morning" (same as "you look tired in it"), "Your personality, I can't help with" (the same "I've picked the outfit" setup as the notice).
- Assumptions: "not a morning person" (the reader's mornings), "never grown anything but tired" (a guess about the reader's life, and too long).
- Cut as not funny: "think about the weather… it won't think about you", "knowing the weather means tomorrow will go well. Adorable", "except you… 'rough.'"
