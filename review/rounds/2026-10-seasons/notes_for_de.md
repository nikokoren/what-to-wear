# Round 13 idea notes for the German writer

These ideas come from the English pool: situations, objects and joke shapes the English
screen has made work that the German pool hasn't touched. They're notes, not lines. The
English wording is left out on purpose, and none of these should come back as a
translation. The brief says no German line may share its joke with an English one, so
take the *situation* and find the Grantler's own joke in it. The English screen brags.
The Grantler grumbles, envies, feels unappreciated, is stingy, always knew it, and
mostly finds fault with other people. Where English says "look how good I am", German
usually wants "and nobody thanks me for it" or "and of course it'll be my fault".

Before you start:
- **What's empty in German right now:** every `alone_*` pool (nice, cool, wet, warming,
  cooling, fickle at both levels; English has 11 lines there), `flavour_10.snow`,
  `flavour_11.evening`, `flavour_vf_10.hot`, `flavour_vf_11.cooling` and
  `flavour_vf_11.warming`. `flavour_11.on` has a single line (English has 5).
- **Standard German, not Austrian** (no "eh", "nix", "wurscht", "Leut'"), no rhymes, no
  words the owner doesn't know.
- **Don't assume a wall,** no clothing advice, and evening lines must not guess the
  reader's plans.

## D1. Air conditioning, draughts and the train with the broken AC
- **Idea:** English has the reader about to get strong opinions on air conditioning (kept
  twice). In Germany that topic is a culture of its own: AC "makes you ill", the fear of
  draughts (Zugluft), the office fight over the window, and every summer the news story
  about a train whose air conditioning has failed.
- **Where:** `now_10.hot` (drawing: shorts/T-shirt, sun).
- **Why:** concrete scenes everyone has seen are what earn German stars (fans sold out
  at the hardware store, the smelly train). German hot lines have nothing on AC.
- **Bend:** the Grantler grumbles at both sides: the ones who want it colder and the ones
  who fear a cold from it. Or he gripes about the train being an oven again.
- **Trap:** don't name the rail company if it reads as a brand attack. "Die Bahn" is
  already in the pool, so it's fine as a general term.

## D2. Pretending you'll do some exercise
- **Idea:** on a perfect day the reader *talks* about going for a run, or takes one
  short walk and talks about it for a week.
- **Where:** `now_11.nice` (drawing: light outfit, sun).
- **Why:** this is the most-starred English idea (six starred votes over several
  wordings). The German 11-nice pool has nothing about the reader's good intentions.
- **Bend:** German has its own way into this: the "one really ought to…" (man müsste mal
  wieder…) that never gets done, running shoes bought in January still in the box, a
  Sunday stroll filed as a week's worth of sport, or functional outdoor clothing worn to
  the bakery.
- **Trap:** a German line about *other people* jogging to be seen was vetoed. Keep it on
  the reader. The bike-not-train line was vetoed too.

## D3. Follow-up for "a layer goes on later": you'll take the credit
- **Idea:** the reader will put the jacket on later and act as if it were their own idea.
- **Where:** `flavour_11.on`, under "Nimm eine Jacke mit, heute Abend kühlt's ab" and
  the other "on" facts. The group has only one German line.
- **Why:** the owner named the English version as a follow-up that "works", one that
  picks up the fact line and twists it (round 11).
- **Bend:** this suits the Grantler better than the screen. He's the one who said it
  this morning and will never get the credit. The resentment of the unthanked weather
  man.
- **Trap:** German follow-ups about freezing later and "acting surprised" were vetoed
  twice as not flowing. The credit is the joke here, not the cold.

## D4. Halloween: his face is costume enough
- **Idea:** the English screen says its plain everyday look already counts as a Halloween
  costume.
- **Where:** `flavour_10.theme_spooky` (German has one line). Theme lines show on steady
  days under the forecast. No weather words.
- **Why:** English has three spooky lines at 10 and they're kept. The German Krampus line
  of the same shape (finally someone in a worse mood than me) is starred.
- **Bend:** the Grantler is the scary old neighbour children avoid all year. On
  Halloween his scowl is finally in season and he needs no costume. Or the children are
  more scared of him than of any mask.
- **Trap:** the vetoed line about unknown children ringing the bell "has the right
  direction, doesn't land". Don't redo the doorbell. German already has the "back when it
  was Reformation Day" grumble, so don't do Halloween-is-American again.

## D5. Being right is the only joy he has left
- **Idea:** English has a standalone line where the screen says being right later in the
  day is the best part of its day, and another where it saw the cooling coming.
- **Where:** `alone_10.warming` and `alone_10.cooling` (both empty in German). These
  show as standalone lines under the drawing on a day that warms (a layer comes off later)
  or cools (a layer is needed later). They must not depend on the forecast line being
  visible.
- **Why:** it fills an empty slot. Being right (Rechthaben) is also the German grumbler's
  hobby: the pleasure of saying "told you so" at five in the afternoon.
- **Bend:** sour pleasure, not pride. He doesn't enjoy the sun, but he enjoys having
  been right about it.
- **Trap:** a German line about finding a new thing to grumble about was vetoed ("write
  new, robotic"). Keep it concrete about the change in the day.

## D6. The device that uses no electricity
- **Idea:** English brags that e-ink holds its picture all night without power and
  gives off no blue light.
- **Where:** `now_10.night` or `now_10.evening`.
- **Why:** English has several kept lines in night/evening on this and German has none.
  The fact is real and specific to the device.
- **Bend:** the Grantler as a penny-pincher. He's the only thing in the flat not
  driving up the electricity bill, and he grumbles at the standby lights, the TV left on
  in an empty room and the charger left in the socket. It borders on the parent's
  classic about not paying to light the street.
- **Trap:** the owner wants the electricity fact to stay accurate. E-ink uses power only
  when it changes the picture.

## D7. My battery feels it coming, like old knees
- **Idea:** English says the screen's battery hates the cold too (kept).
- **Where:** `now_10.cold`, or `now_10.wet` for rain.
- **Why:** the old man who feels the weather in his joints before any forecast
  (wetterfühlig, "ich spür's in den Knien") is a German type. Here the device has a
  battery instead of knees. It's the Grantler and the screen in one line.
- **Bend:** he complains about his battery the way pensioners complain about their
  rheumatism. Or he claims he didn't need a forecast because his battery told him.
- **Trap:** a German line calling the *reader* weather-sensitive whenever it suits them was
  vetoed. Keep the aches on the screen.

## D8. The Christmas tree on the pavement (seasonal, January)
- **Idea:** English says the screen will still be up in February, unlike the tree.
- **Where:** `now_10.cold`, `now_10.wet` or `now_10.cool` with `{M:01}`. All three moods
  happen in January, so the line must not depend on the weather.
- **Why:** January has no German line yet. The German scene is better than the English
  one: discarded trees lying on the pavement for weeks, needles in the carpet until
  Easter, the neighbour's fairy lights still up in March.
- **Bend:** a pure grumble about the neighbours' sloppiness, or a hedged guess that the
  reader's tree is still standing.
- **Trap:** a theme line can't run in January (the themes end on Christmas Day and New
  Year). This has to be a month-tagged now line.

## D9. Christmas sweets in the shops in September (seasonal)
- **Idea:** English has the reader claiming the Christmas cookies were for guests.
  The German angle is a different and very German complaint: Lebkuchen and Spekulatius
  on the supermarket shelves while it's still warm out.
- **Where:** `now_10.nice`, `now_10.hot` or `now_10.cool` with `{M:09}` (or `{M:09,10}`).
- **Why:** a yearly grumble everyone in Germany knows. It's a concrete scene, and no line
  in either language covers September.
- **Bend:** the Grantler is outraged on principle and buys them anyway.
- **Trap:** it reads best on a warm day. Avoid the snow and cold pools.

## D10. The Christmas cookie tin "for visitors" (seasonal, December)
- **Idea:** the reader says the Christmas biscuits were for visitors, and somehow the
  tin is empty before anyone visits. This one is close to the English cookie idea, so the
  German joke has to come from the Advent baking ritual, not from the excuse.
- **Where:** `now_11.cold`, `now_11.wet` or `now_11.cool` with `{M:12}`, or
  `flavour_11.theme_xmas`.
- **Why:** Advent baking is bigger in Germany than in English-speaking homes. The German
  pool has Glühwein for December but nothing about baking.
- **Bend:** the Grantler counts the biscuits, or notes that the dough was gone before
  the oven was hot.
- **Trap:** don't reuse the English punch line about there being no guests. Find the
  German one (the tin, the varieties, Oma's recipe nobody gets right).

## D11. No signal (Funkloch)
- **Idea:** English says the screen would come along but only works within WiFi range.
- **Where:** `now_10.nice` (everyone heads out to the lake, the hills or the beer
  garden), or `flavour_10.hot`.
- **Why:** the English line is in the pool. In Germany, dead spots in mobile coverage on
  the train and in the countryside are a national running joke.
- **Bend:** he stays home where the WiFi works, and out there they'll be holding their
  phones up to the sky in search of one bar.
- **Trap:** keep the line about people, not about networks. Name no provider.

## D12. Follow-up for "a layer comes off": the highlight of your day
- **Idea:** taking the jacket off is the most exciting thing that will happen to the
  reader today.
- **Where:** `flavour_11.off`, under "Gegen Mittag kann die Jacke weg".
- **Why:** starred in English, with the owner's note that sarcasm and forecast work well
  together here. The German off-11 lines are all about knotting sleeves and forgetting the
  jacket.
- **Bend:** make it personal: the reader's life, week or social calendar is so empty
  that the jacket coming off counts as news.
- **Trap:** the impersonal German version (that's all the excitement today) was vetoed
  as "not funny". Land it on the reader.

## D13. Evening follow-up: nobody listens to me anyway
- **Idea:** English has the reader reading the whole tip and then doing what they were
  going to do anyway (starred).
- **Where:** `flavour_11.evening`, which is empty in German. It sits under "Was du
  anhast, passt für den ganzen Abend".
- **Why:** owner-starred, and it fills an empty slot. Being ignored is the Grantler's
  natural state.
- **Bend:** resigned grumbling. He could tell you anything, and the evening will go as it
  goes. He doesn't know why he bothers.
- **Trap:** evening rules: no going out, no going home, no plans. "Talking to the wall"
  idioms are risky because a German line about talking to the wall was vetoed, and the
  owner dislikes wall references.

## D14. Other frames get to show nice pictures
- **Idea:** English envies screens that get to show a Monet while it shows the weather.
- **Where:** `alone_10.cool` (empty in German). It shows on an ordinary cool day under
  the drawing.
- **Why:** three English lines on this are kept, and the cool mood is "the weak mood" in
  both languages. A self-pity line gives it something to push against.
- **Bend:** the Grantler's self-pity is about his lot. Other frames in other flats show
  holiday photos and grandchildren. He gets your weather and has to watch you leave
  every morning.
- **Trap:** the firmware-update boredom angle is English territory, and German "dull
  weather" similes (waiting room, tap water) were vetoed. Make it envy, not boredom.

## D15. The world goes black and white, and suddenly it's pretty
- **Idea:** English points out that snow turns the world outside into the screen's own
  black and white.
- **Where:** `now_10.snow`, or the empty `flavour_10.snow` under "{WHENP} fängt's an zu
  schneien".
- **Why:** this is a real feature of the device that no German line uses. Snow lines
  are the German pool's strongest area, but all of them are about public chaos.
- **Bend:** the double standard. When the world goes grey and white everyone calls it
  magical and takes photos. When he looks like that every day, people ask why the screen
  isn't in colour.
- **Trap:** German vetoes on the drawing (snow looks tidy in my pictures, I've got the
  white weather map) are a warning. Make it about people, not the drawing.

## D16. Only looked at when it rains
- **Idea:** English says a chance of rain suddenly gets everyone checking the screen
  (starred), and that on sunny days people walk past it (kept).
- **Where:** `alone_10.wet` (empty in German), or `now_10.nice` for the sunny side.
- **Why:** it's starred in English and the two empty slots need standalone lines. The
  German rain-radar line covers the staring but not the screen's hurt feelings.
- **Bend:** the fair-weather friend complaint. Nobody notices him all summer, but as
  soon as there's a cloud he's everyone's best friend, and if they get wet it's his fault.
- **Trap:** "it'll be my fault again" is already starred in German. Use the attention
  angle, not the blame.

## D17. The umbrella economy at the station exit
- **Idea:** English says the screen is very good for umbrella sales (starred).
- **Where:** `now_10.wet` (drawing: umbrella).
- **Why:** starred, and a concrete German scene follows naturally: the cheap kiosk
  umbrella that flips inside out at the first gust, the sellers who appear at the
  station exit as soon as it rains, and the price that doubles.
- **Bend:** the Grantler as an expert on bad umbrellas who knows how many days they last.
- **Trap:** stay away from pavement umbrella etiquette (the biggest umbrella wins). A
  German line on that was vetoed as too long.

## D18. Gloves off for the touchscreen
- **Idea:** English says the reader takes their gloves off every time to use the phone
  (kept).
- **Where:** `now_10.cold` as a public scene, or `now_11.cold` about the reader.
- **Why:** German cold lines have no gloves-and-gadgets scene. The German version has
  extra material: the ticket machine, the parking meter, the self-checkout.
- **Bend:** he watches a queue at the ticket machine, each person pulling off a glove
  with their teeth.
- **Trap:** a German line saying "your gloves are still in the basement" was vetoed.
  Don't do the forgotten-gloves angle.

## D19. Beauty sleep, the Grantler version
- **Idea:** English has the screen bragging that it doesn't need beauty sleep (kept twice).
- **Where:** `now_10.night` (after 22:00, bed allowed).
- **Why:** the night pool is all fridge and episodes at 11. At 10 it's the neighbours.
  A line about the screen itself would add range.
- **Bend:** flip the vanity into self-deprecation. For him, sleep stopped helping years
  ago. Or he grumbles that young people need beauty sleep and still look like that.
- **Trap:** don't mirror the English punch line, which is vanity. The Grantler's
  version runs the other way.

## Don't carry these over
- **Thanksgiving, trick-or-treat candy, pumpkin spice:** American. Erntedankfest (harvest
  festival) has no theme key.
- **"Crisp", "fresh", calling yourself outdoorsy:** the English euphemism joke. The German
  "angenehm frisch" version was vetoed.
- **The firmware-update and "thrilling stuff" boredom jokes, and drawing-workload
  jokes about snow or fewer clothes:** vetoed in German already (waiting room, tap water,
  "I don't draw any further down").
- **Tomorrow-you vs tonight-you, the snooze button:** the German snooze line was vetoed.
